# Global Economy Pool System

> Replaces the per-city resource pool model with player-scoped global pools for funds and manpower. Reduces micromanagement while maintaining strategic depth.

## Overview

The global economy pool system consolidates all resource generation and consumption into two player-scoped pools:

| Pool | Purpose | Generated By | Consumed By |
|------|---------|--------------|-------------|
| **Funds** (`HashMap<String, u32>`) | Currency for recruitment, upgrades, equipment | City income (per economy tick) | Unit recruitment, city upgrades, equipment resupply |
| **Manpower** (`HashMap<String, u32>`) | Human resources for building and maintaining armies | City manpower output (per economy tick) | Unit recruitment, unit resupply (men), unit healing, constant attrition |

Both pools are unbounded (no maximum). They grow with each owned city and shrink with army demands. The system creates a natural tension between territorial expansion (more cities = more resources) and military ambition (more units = more consumption).

## Resource Generation

### Funds

Generated every economy tick (every 10 ticks by default) for each owned city:

```
funds_per_city = GameSettings.funds_per_city  (default: 1.0)
income_multiplier = 1.0 + (city.level - 1) * income_growth_per_level
funds_gain = funds_per_city * income_multiplier
```

**Example** (default settings, `income_growth_per_level = 0.5`):
| City Level | Income Multiplier | Funds/Tick |
|------------|-------------------|------------|
| 1 | 1.0 | 1 |
| 2 | 1.5 | 1 |
| 3 | 2.0 | 2 |
| 5 | 3.0 | 3 |
| 10 | 5.5 | 5 |

### Manpower

Generated every tick for each owned city:

```
manpower_gain = manpower_regen_per_tick * city.level
```

**Default**: `manpower_regen_per_tick = 10.0`

**Example** (default settings):
| City Level | Manpower/Tick | Manpower/Second (at 60fps) |
|------------|---------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | 10 | 600 |
| 3 | 30 | 1800 |
| 5 | 50 | 3000 |

## Resource Consumption

### Unit Recruitment

Recruiting a unit deducts from both pools:
- **Manpower**: `unit_type.men` soldiers
- **Funds**: `unit_type.recruitment_cost`

If either pool is insufficient, recruitment fails and manpower is refunded.

### Unit Resupply (Supply System)

Units within supply range of a friendly city receive manpower resupply each tick:
- **Rate**: `recruitment_rate` per tick from the closest city
- **Multi-city bonus**: Additional cities contribute at a decayed rate

```
total_rate = recruitment_rate + (cities_count - 1) * recruitment_rate * secondary_city_decay
men_gain = total_rate.min(unit.max_men - unit.men)
```

The total `men_gain` is deducted from the player's global manpower pool.

### Constant Manpower Attrition

Every unit loses manpower continuously regardless of combat status:

```
manpower_drain = 1.0 / 600.0  // ~0.00167 men per tick
```

**Impact**: A unit of 1000 men loses ~1 man every 600 ticks (~10 seconds at 60fps). This creates a steady economic cost for maintaining standing armies and encourages tactical engagement over positional warfare.

### Starvation Attrition

When a unit's equipment drops below 90% of its needed amount:
- Additional manpower loss: `attrition_rate * 3.0` per tick
- This accelerates the drain significantly, making supply lines critical

## Supply Range

Each city has a `supply_range` that grows with level:

```
supply_range = base_supply_range + (level - 1) * supply_range_growth
```

**Default values**:
| Setting | Value |
|---------|-------|
| `base_supply_range` | 5.0 |
| `supply_range_growth` | 3.0 |
| `secondary_city_decay` | 0.5 |

**Example** (default settings):
| City Level | Supply Range | Coverage Radius (grid cells) |
|------------|-------------|------------------------------|
| 1 | 5.0 | 5 cells |
| 2 | 8.0 | 8 cells |
| 3 | 11.0 | 11 cells |
| 5 | 17.0 | 17 cells |

Units must be within a city's supply range (Euclidean distance check) to receive resupply.

## Configuration

All economy parameters are configurable via `CityUpgradeSettings` in `src/config.rs`:

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|-------|------|---------|-------------|
| `enabled` | `bool` | `false` | Master switch for city upgrades/supply |
| `max_level` | `u32` | `100` | Maximum city level |
| `base_upgrade_cost` | `u32` | `200` | Cost to upgrade = `base * level` |
| `income_growth_per_level` | `f32` | `0.5` | Income multiplier growth per level |
| `base_supply_range` | `f32` | `5.0` | Level 1 supply range |
| `supply_range_growth` | `f32` | `3.0` | Additional range per level |
| `secondary_city_decay` | `f32` | `0.5` | Decay for non-closest city resupply |
| `manpower_regen_per_tick` | `f32` | `10.0` | Manpower per city level per tick |

And from `GameSettings`:

| Field | Type | Default | Description |
|-------|------|---------|-------------|
| `funds_per_city` | `f32` | `1.0` | Base funds generated per city per tick |
| `recruitment_rate` | `f32` | `1.0` | Manpower resupply rate per tick |

## System Interactions

### Economy System (`src/game/systems/economy.rs`)

- Runs every economy tick (default: every 10 ticks)
- Iterates owned cities, generates funds and manpower into global pools
- Returns a map of `player_id -> funds_earned` for stats tracking

### Supply System (`src/game/systems/supply.rs`)

- Runs every game tick
- Uses a **three-phase pattern** to avoid borrow conflicts:
  1. **Snapshot**: Collect city positions and supply ranges
  2. **Plan**: Identify units needing resupply and compute deductions
  3. **Apply**: Deduct from global pools, then apply to ECS entities
- Uses pre-computed `TickContext.units_by_player` for efficient iteration

### Attrition System (`src/game/systems/attrition.rs`)

- Runs every game tick
- Applies constant manpower drain (`1/600` per tick) to all units
- Handles equipment attrition, fighting penalties, and starvation
- Uses two-phase pattern: query + collect changes → apply mutations in batch

### Recruitment (`src/game/game.rs`)

- On-demand (triggered by player/LLM action)
- Validates city ownership, checks both pools, deducts, then spawns unit
- Atomic: if funds are insufficient after manpower is deducted, manpower is refunded

### City Upgrades

- Spends funds to increase city level
- Higher level = more supply range, more income multiplier, more manpower generation
- Cost scales linearly: `upgrade_cost = base_upgrade_cost * level`

## Design Decisions

### Why Global Pools Instead of Per-City Pools?

1. **Reduced micromanagement**: Players don't need to assign specific units to specific cities for resupply. Any city in range contributes.
2. **Simplified economy model**: Resources flow from "empire" to "army" rather than tracking per-city production/consumption chains.
3. **Strategic depth preserved**: City count and level still matter (they determine total output). Supply range still creates positioning incentives.
4. **Encourages territorial expansion**: More cities = larger pool = larger armies. The incentive structure remains similar.

### Why Constant Attrition?

Without constant attrition, a player could raise a large army and then hold it indefinitely at zero cost. The `1/600` drain ensures:
- Large standing armies are economically expensive
- There's pressure to use forces rather than stockpile them
- Players must balance army size against sustainable economy

### Why No Manpower Maximum?

A capped pool creates artificial scarcity that doesn't map to the intended design. An unbounded pool means:
- Generation rate matters more than storage capacity
- Surplus manpower during peacetime can be spent on a war effort
- No need to manage "storage" mechanics

### Why Two Separate Pools (Funds + Manpower)?

Separating the pools creates distinct strategic decisions:
- **Funds** control how many units you can *buy* (recruitment cost)
- **Manpower** controls how many soldiers you can *field and maintain* (recruitment + attrition + resupply)

A player with high funds but low manpower can recruit expensive units but struggle to keep them at strength. A player with high manpower but low funds can maintain existing units but can't field new ones.

## Balance Considerations

### Economy vs Army Size Equilibrium

The steady-state army a player can sustain depends on:

```
total_manpower_gen = Σ(manpower_regen_per_tick * city_level)
total_manpower_drain = Σ(unit.men / 600) + resupply_rate * units_in_range
```

At equilibrium: `total_manpower_gen ≈ total_manpower_drain`

For default settings (10 MP/tick per city level, 1000-men units):
- 1 city (level 1) → ~600 MP/tick → can sustain ~360,000 men → ~360 units
- This is intentionally generous to allow for combat losses and attrition spikes

### Supply Range as Positioning Mechanic

Supply range creates natural fronts:
- Units beyond supply range can't be resupplied
- Capturing enemy cities extends your supply lines
- Destroying/capturing a supply hub can strand enemy forces

The `secondary_city_decay` parameter allows tuning:
- `0.0` = only closest city matters (encourages forward bases)
- `1.0` = all cities contribute equally (encourages depth)
